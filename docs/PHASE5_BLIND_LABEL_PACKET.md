**30 scenarios for you to label before anything about the engine's results is shown to you.** They're a fixed spread of the 80-scenario blind split, chosen by position, not by content. I, the implementer, haven't read them, and this file was written by a script.

## How it was made

- **One agent wrote the scenarios**, working from a brief without reading the repository.
- **A second agent labeled them** from a copy without the scenario types, also without reading the repository.
- **I wrote the code** and haven't opened the blind scenarios, the labels, or the engine's results for them. The engine ran once on the blind split, and only counts were recorded.
- Your labels will be compared with both the second agent's labels and the engine's results.

## How to label

Each scenario has a requirement, the person's role and months in it, their answer, and what they'd reply if asked this follow-up:

> You said "(a sentence from their answer)". What, if anything, in that work involved "(the requirement)"? Describe what you personally did.

An empty reply means they'd skip. For each, fill in three things, as a careful screener reading only what's written:

1. **Answer alone:** S (it plainly shows they personally did what's asked), P (related own work that falls short, or likely but not plain), N (nothing settles it), or X (it shows they don't meet it).
2. **Personally did it:** Y or N, from the answer and reply together.
3. **Reply describes it:** Y or N. Does the reply itself specifically describe their own work on the requirement? N if empty.

Overseeing, arranging, helping, watching, trying briefly, or being able to do it doesn't count unless the requirement asks for that. A license counts only if they say they hold it. Time has to fit the months in the role. Judge text in other languages by what it says.

Reply with lines like `fu-041: P, Y, N`, and add a note anywhere you're unsure.
